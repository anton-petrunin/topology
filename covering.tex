\chapter{Coverings}\label{chap:coverings}

In the previous chapter we introduced a map $w\:\RR\to \SS^1$,
defined by $w(x)=\exp(2\cdot\pi\cdot i\cdot x)$,
and the special properties of this map were used to calculate fundamental group of $\SS^1$.
Now we will study covering maps --- the special type of maps that have the key properties of $w\:\RR\z\to \SS^1$.
Covering maps will be used to calculate fundamental groups of other spaces,
but more importantly, they will give another connection between algebra and geometry that has applications from both sides.

\section{Definition}

\begin{thm}{Definition}\label{def:evenly-covered}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ be a continuous map.

An open set $W\subset \c{X}$ is called \index{evenly covered open set}\emph{evenly covered} by $w$ if
\[w^{-1}(W)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in \c{I}}V_\alpha,\]
where $V_\alpha$ are pairwise disjoint open sets in $\tilde{\c{X}}$ such that restriction
$w|_{V_\alpha}$ is a homeomorphism $V_\alpha\to W$ for each $\alpha\in \c{I}$.
The sets $V_\alpha$ are called \index{sheet}\emph{sheets} over $W$.

If for every point $x\in \c{X}$ there is an open neighborhood $W\ni x$ that is evenly covered by $w$, then $w$ is a \index{covering map}\emph{covering map}, $\tilde{\c{X}}$ is a \index{covering space}\emph{covering space} of $\c{X}$.
\end{thm}

Note that $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ is a covering if $\c{X}$ admits an open cover by evenly covered sets.
In particular, from the proof of \ref{prop:lifting}, $w(x)=\exp(2\cdot\pi\cdot i\cdot x)$  defines a covering map $\RR\to\SS^1$ --- this map and its properties will guide us to study general covering maps.

Most interesting examples of covering maps are surjective and have connected source $\tilde{\c{X}}$ and target $\c{X}$.
Many authors add these conditions to the definition of covering.
I might silently assume this, but formally speaking it is not required (in this text).
For example, one may take several disjoint copies of space $\c{X}$ as $\tilde{\c{X}}$ and map a point in one copy to the corresponding point in $\c{X}$ --- this is the so-called \index{trivial covering}\emph{trivial} covering map.

The following observation says that a covering map must be open.
Note that restriction of the map $w\:\RR\to \SS^1$ to a bounded open interval provides an example of open map which is not covering; so the converse does not hold in general.


\begin{thm}{Observation}\label{thm:covering-open}
Any covering map is an open map.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ be a covering map.
Choose an open set $V\subset \tilde{\c{X}}$ and a point $\tilde x\in V$.

Choose an evenly covered open set $W\ni x=w(\tilde x)$; let $\{V_\alpha\}$ be a family of its sheets.
Since $\tilde x\in w^{-1}(W)$, we have $\tilde x\in V_\alpha$ for some $\alpha$.
Let $V'_\alpha=V_\alpha\cap V$.
Since $w|_{V_\alpha}$ is a homeomorphism, $W'=w(V_\alpha')$ is open in $W$, and (since $W$ is open) $W'$ is open in $\c{X}$.
That is, any $x\in w(V)$ admits an open neighborhood $W'\subset w(V)$;
in other words, $w(V)$ is open.
\qeds

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:covering-clopen}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to\c{X}$ be a covering map.
Show that the image $w(\tilde{\c{X}})$ is clopen set in $\c{X}$.
Conclude that, if $\c{X}$ is connected, and $\tilde{\c{X}}\ne\emptyset$, then $w$ is surjective.
\end{thm}

Let $n$ be an integer.
A covering map $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ is called \emph{$n$-fold} if inverse image of every point $x\in \c{X}$ has exactly $n$ points.

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:n-folded}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ is a covering map, and let $n$ be an integer.
Assume that $\c{X}$ is connected, and an inverse image of some point $x\in \c{X}$ has exactly $n$ points.
Show that $w$ is $n$-fold.
\end{thm}

\section{Path liftings}

Recall that in Section~\ref{sec:lifting-s1}, we defined lifts with respect to the covering $\RR\to\SS^1$.
Now we repeat the same definition for general continuous map.

Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ and $f\:\c{Y}\to \c{X}$ be continuous maps.
A continuous map $\tilde f\:\c{Y}\to \tilde{\c{X}}$ is called a \index{lift}\emph{lift} of $f$ (with respect to $w$) if $w\circ \tilde f=f$;
in other words, the following diagram commutes:
\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}
& \tilde{\c{X}} \arrow[d, "w"]\\
\c{Y} \arrow[r, "f"']\arrow[ru, "\tilde f"] & \c{X}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{figure}

In this case we also say that $f$ \index{lift}\emph{lifts} to $\tilde{\c{X}}$.
In particular, if $\c{Y}=[0,1]$, then $f$ is a path, and the path $\tilde f\:[0,1]\to \tilde{\c{X}}$ is a \emph{lift} of $f$.

The following lemma is a direct generalization of \ref{prop:lifting}, and it can be proved along the same lines.
In fact, definition of covering map was made just for this lemma.

\begin{thm}{Lemma}\label{lem:path-lifting}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ be a covering map, and let $a_0\:[0,1]\to \c{X}$ be a path.
Suppose that $\tilde x_0\in \tilde{\c{X}}$ and $x_0\in \c{X}$ be a points such that $w(\tilde x_0)=x_0=a_0(0)$.
Then there is unique lift $\tilde a_0$ of $a$ such that $\tilde a_0(0)=\tilde x_0$.

Moreover, given a homotopy of paths $a_\tau$ from $a_0$ to $a_1$ there is unique lifted homotopy $\tilde a_\tau$ such that $\tilde a_0(0)=\tilde x_0$.
In particular, if $a_0\sim a_1$ then $\tilde a_0(1)= \tilde a_1(1)$.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:bijection-fiber}
Let $w\: \tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ be a covering.
Assume that $\tilde{\c{X}}$ is simply connected.
Then, for any $x_0\in \c{X}$ there is a bijection $f$ between the fundamental group $\pi_1(\c{X},x_0)$ and the inverse image $w^{-1}\{x_0\}\subset \tilde{\c{X}}$.
Moreover, $f$ can be constructed in the following way:

Choose $\tilde x_0\in \tilde{\c{X}}$ such that $w(\tilde x_0)=x_0$.
Given $\alpha\in \pi_1(\c{X},x_0)$, choose a loop $a$ based at $x_0\in \c{X}$ such that $\alpha=[a]$.
Consider a lift $\tilde a$ of $a$ such $\tilde a(0)=\tilde x_0$ and let $f(\alpha)= \tilde a(1)$.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Note that $w(\tilde a(1))=a(1)=x_0$; therefore $f(\alpha)\in w^{-1}\{x_0\}$ as required.

Let us show that the map $f$ is well defined;
that is, the point $\tilde a(1)$ is the same for any loop $a$ such that $\alpha=[a]$.
Indeed, if $\alpha=[b]$ for some loop $b$, then $a\sim b$, and by the second statement in the lemma, we get $\tilde a(1)=\tilde b(1)$.

Since $\tilde{\c{X}}$ is simply connected, there is a path $\tilde a$ from $\tilde x_0$ to any point in $w^{-1}\{x_0\}$.
Note that $a=w\circ\tilde a$ is a loop in $\c{X}$ with base point $x_0$, and $\tilde a$ is the lift of $a$ as in the construction.
This implies that the map $f\:\pi_1(\c{X},x_0)\to w^{-1}\{x_0\}$ is onto.

It remains to check that $f$ is injective.
Suppose $\tilde a(1)=\tilde b(1)$ for two paths starting at $\tilde x_0$.
Since $\tilde{\c{X}}$ is simply connected,
we have that $\tilde a\sim \tilde b$ (see \ref{ex:sc=homot-paths}); that is, there is a homotopy $\tilde a_\tau$ from $\tilde a$ to $\tilde b$.
Observe that $a_\tau=w\circ  \tilde a_\tau$ is a homotopy from $a=w\circ \tilde a$ to $b=w\circ \tilde b$;
that is, $[a]=[b]\z\in \pi_1(\c{X},x_0)$.
\qeds


\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:induced-hom-covering}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{X}}\to \c{X}$ be a covering map.
Suppose that $\tilde x_0\in \tilde{\c{X}}$ and $x_0\in \c{X}$ be a points such that $w(\tilde x_0)=x_0$.

\begin{subthm}{ex:induced-hom-covering:injective}
Show that $w_*\:\pi_1(\tilde{\c{X}},\tilde x_0)\to \pi_1(\c{X}, x_0)$ is a \index{monomorphism}\emph{monomorphism} (which means injective homomorphism).
\end{subthm}

\begin{subthm}{ex:induced-hom-covering:lift}
Let $b$ be a loop in $\c{X}$ with base point $x_0$ and $\tilde b$ be its lift in $\tilde{\c{X}}$ such that $\tilde b(0)=\tilde x_0$.
Show that $\tilde b(1)=\tilde x_0$ if and only if $[b]\in \Im w_*$.
\end{subthm}

\begin{subthm}{ex:induced-hom-covering:action}
Show that the following construction defines an action $\pi_1(\c{X},x_0)$ on $w^{-1}\{x_0\}$.
Given $\alpha\in \pi_1(\c{X},x_0)$, choose a loop $a$ based at $x_0\in \c{X}$ such that $\alpha=[a]$.
Given a point $p\in w^{-1}\{x_0\}$ consider a lift $\tilde a$ of $a$ such $\tilde a(0)=p$ and define $\alpha\cdot p\df\tilde a(1)$.
\end{subthm}


\end{thm}

\section{Freely discontinuous action}

A continuous action $G\acts \c{X}$ is called \index{freely discontinuous action}\emph{freely discontinuous} if for $x\in\c{X}$ there is an open neighborhood $V \ni x$ such that
$V\cap g\cdot V=\emptyset$ for any $g\ne 1$.
Since $g_1\cdot V\cap g_2\cdot V=g_1\cdot (V\cap g_1^{-1}\cdot g_2\cdot V)$ we also have that $g_1\cdot V\cap g_2\cdot V=\emptyset$ if $g_1\ne g_2$.

\begin{thm}{Proposition}\label{prop:freely-discontinuous}
If $G\acts \c{X}$ is a freely discontinuous action, then the quotient map $w\:\c{X}\to \c{X}/G$ is a covering.
\end{thm}

The covering maps that can be realized as quotient map for a freely discontinuous action of some group $G$ are called \index{regular covering}\emph{regular},
or, if one needs to specify the group \index{$G$-covering}\emph{$G$-covering}.

\parit{Proof.}
By the definition of the quotient map, $w\:\c{X}\to \c{X}/G$ is continuous and surjective;
moreover, by \ref{ex:quotient-map:open}, $w$ is open.
Let $V$ be an open neighborhood of $x\in \c{X}$ that meets the condition in the definition of freely discontinuous action.

Since $w$ is open, $W=w(V)$ is an open neighborhood of $w(x)\z=[x]=G\cdot x$.
Furthermore,
\[w^{-1}(W)=G\cdot V=\bigcup_{g\in G} g\cdot V.\]
and the restriction $w|_{g\cdot V}$ is a homeomorphism $g\cdot V\to W$ (since $w|_{g\cdot V}$ is open, continuous, and bijective), so $w$ meets the conditions in the definition of covering map.
\qeds

Note that the map $w\:\RR\to \SS^1$ constructed in \ref{sec:lifting-s1} is the quotient map for a freely discontinuous action $\ZZ\acts\RR$.

An action $G\acts \c{X}$ is called \index{free action}\emph{free} if $g\cdot x\ne x$ for any $x\in\c{X}$ and $g\ne 1$.
Note that any freely discontinuous action is free, but not the other way around;
for example, the action of the additive group $\RR$ by shifts on itself if free, but not freely discontinuous.

\begin{thm}{Corollary}\label{cor:covering-finite-action}
Let $G\acts \c{X}$ be a continuous free action of a finite group $G$.
Assume that $\c{X}$ is Hausdorff.
Then the quotient map $\c{X}\to \c{X}/G$ is a covering.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Choose $x\in \c{X}$ and $g\ne 1$ in $G$.
Since $G\acts \c{X}$ is free, $g\cdot x\ne x$.
Since $\c{X}$ is Hausdorff we can choose open disjoint sets $V_g\ni x$ and $W_g\z\ni g\cdot x$.
We can assume that $W_g=g\cdot V_g$; if not pass to the intersections $V_g\cap g^{-1}\cdot W_g\ni x$ and
$g\cdot (V_g\cap g^{-1}\cdot W_g)=g\cdot V_g\cap W_g\ni g\cdot x$.
Now consider the finite intersection
\[V=\bigcap_{g\ne 1}V_g\ni x,\]
which is open.

Note that $g\cdot V$ are disjoint for all $g\in G$;
that is, the action $G\acts \c{X}$ is freely discontinuous.
It remains to apply \ref{prop:freely-discontinuous}.
\qeds

\section{Real projective plane}

The equivalence class of an integer $m$ modulo $n$ will be denoted by $[m]_n$;
that is
\[[m]_n =\set{m+k\cdot n}{k\in\ZZ}.\]
The set of equivalence class of integers modulo $n$ will be denoted by $\ZZ_n$.
The operation $[m_1]_n+[m_2]_n\df[m_1+m_2]_n$ defines on $\ZZ_n$ a group structure with neutral element $[0]_n$;
it is cyclic group of order $n$, generated by element $[1]_n$.
Recall that all cyclic groups of order $n$ are isomorphic, so it is acceptable to denote a cyclic groups of order $n$ by $\ZZ_n$ (everyone does it, altho formally speaking $\ZZ_n$ is just an example of cyclic groups of order $n$).

Consider the action $\ZZ_2\acts\SS^2$ generated by the involution $x\mapsto -x$.
The quotient space is called \emph{real projective plane} and denoted by $\RP^2$.
Observe that the action is free.
Since $\ZZ_2$ is finite, by the corollary, the quotient map $\SS^2\to \RP^2$ is a covering.
By \ref{ex:van-kamp:s2}, $\SS^2$ is simply connected.
By \ref{thm:bijection-fiber}, $\pi_1(\RP^2)$ has exactly two elements, and therefore it is isomorphic to $\ZZ_2$.
Thus we proved the following.

\begin{thm}{Claim}
The fundamental group of real projective plane is isomorphic to $\ZZ_2$.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:2-folded}
Show that any 2-fold covering is a regular $\ZZ_2$-covering.
\end{thm}

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:2-folded-s1}
Let $\ZZ_2\acts \SS^1$ be the action generated by involution $x\mapsto -x$.
Show that $\SS^1/\ZZ_2\simeq\SS^1$ and therefore the quotient map defines a 2-fold cover $\SS^1\to \SS^1$.
\end{thm}

\section{Borsuk--Ulam theorem}

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:Borsuk-Ulam}
There is no continuous map $\tilde h\:\SS^2\to \SS^1$ such that $\tilde h(-\tilde x)=-\tilde h(\tilde x)$ for any $\tilde x\in\SS^2$.
\end{thm}

The proof relies on the following algebraic lemma.

\begin{thm}{Lemma}
Any homomorphism $\ZZ_2\to\ZZ$ is \index{trivial homomorphism}\emph{trivial};
that is, it maps the whole group to the neutral element.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Assume $\phi\:\ZZ_2\to\ZZ$ is a nontrivial homomorphism,
then $\phi[1]_2\z=n$ for an integer $n\ne 0$.
But then
\[
0=\phi([0]_2)=\phi([1]_2+[1]_2)=\phi([1]_2)+\phi([1]_2) =n+n\ne 0
\]
--- a contradiction.
\qeds


\parit{Proof of \ref{thm:Borsuk-Ulam}.}
Assume that $\tilde h$ exists.
Defining and explaining the following commutative diagram will be the main part of the proof.

\begin{figure}[h!]
\vskip-3mm
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep={1em,2em}, row sep={.5em,1em}]
&\SS^2
\arrow[rr,"\tilde h"]
\arrow[dd,"v"']
&&\SS^1
\arrow[dd,"w"]
\\
&&|[shift={(0,-10pt)}]|[0,1]
\arrow[ul,"\tilde a"' ]
\arrow[ur,"\tilde b" ]
\arrow[dl,"a"' ]
\arrow[dr,"b" ]
&&&
\\
&\RP^2
\arrow[rr,"h"']
&& \SS^1
\end{tikzcd}
\end{figure}

\parit{Path $\tilde a$, loop $a$, and covering map $v$.}
Choose $\tilde x_0\in \SS^2$ and a path $\tilde a\:[0,1]\to \SS^2$ from $\tilde x_0$ to $-\tilde x_0$.
Let $v\: \SS^2\to\RP^2$ be the quotient map for the equivalence relation $\tilde x\sim -\tilde x$.
Note that $v$ which is a 2-fold covering, and $a=v\circ \tilde a$ is a loop in $\RP^2$ based at $x_0=v(\tilde x_0)$, and $\tilde a$ is its lift to $\SS^2$.
In particular, $\alpha=[a]\ne 0\in \ZZ_2=\pi_1(\RP^2)$.

\parit{Path $\tilde b$ and loop $b$, and covering map $w$.}
The path $\tilde b=\tilde h\circ \tilde a$ runs from $\tilde y_0=\tilde h(\tilde x_0)$ to $-\tilde y_0=-f(\tilde x_0)$.
Let $w\: \SS^1\to\SS^1$ be the quotient map for the equivalence relation $\tilde y\sim -\tilde y$, which is also a 2-fold covering map (see \ref{ex:2-folded-s1});
note that $b=w\circ \tilde b$ is a loop in $\SS^1$ based at $y_0=w(\tilde y_0)$ and $\tilde b$ is its lift to $\SS^1$.

\parit{Map $h$.}
Recall that $v\: \SS^2\to\RP^2$ and $w\: \SS^1\to\SS^1$ are quotient maps that identify antipodal points.
Since $\tilde h$ is odd, it maps the equivalence class in $\SS^2$ to an equivalence class in $\SS^1$.
In other words, there is a map $h\:\RP^2\to\SS^1$ such that $h\circ v=w\circ \tilde h$.
This finishes describing the commutative diagram.

\parit{Proof assembling.}
Consider the induced homomorphism
\[h_*\:\pi_1(\RP^2,x_0)\to \pi_1(\SS^1,y_0).\]
Since $\pi_1(\RP^2,x_0)\cong \ZZ_2$ and $\pi_1(\SS^1,y_0)\cong \ZZ$, the lemma implies that $h_*$ is trivial.
On the other hand $h_*[a]=[b]$ and since $\tilde b$ is not a loop, $[b]\ne 1$ --- a contradiction.
\qeds

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:Borsuk-Ulam}

\begin{subthm}{ex:Borsuk-Ulam:Borsuk-Ulam}
Show that for every continuous map $f\: \SS^2\to \RR^2$ there exists a pair of antipodal
points in $\SS^2$ that map to a single point.
\end{subthm}

\begin{subthm}{ex:ABC}
Let $A$, $B$, and $C$ be closed subsets $\SS^2$ such that $A\cup B\cup C=\SS^2$.
Apply \ref{SHORT.ex:Borsuk-Ulam:Borsuk-Ulam} theorem to the map $f\: \SS^2\z\to \RR^2$ defined by
\[f\:x\mapsto (\dist_Ax,\dist_Bx)\]
to conclude that one of the sets $A$, $B$, or $C$
contains a pair of antipodal
points in $\SS^2$.
\end{subthm}

\begin{subthm}{ex:ABCD}
Give an example showing that analogous statement does not hold for four closed set $A$, $B$, $C$, and $D$.
\end{subthm}

\end{thm}
