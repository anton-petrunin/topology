\section{Reasonable spaces}\label{sec:Reasonable spaces}

The term \index{reasonable space}\emph{reasonable space} will be used as a shortcut for \index{locally simply-connected}\emph{locally simply-connected space}.
This term will be used only in these notes; it is not standard and can mean something else elsewhere.

So, a topological space $\c{Z}$ is \index{reasonable space}\emph{reasonable} if for any open set $V\subset \c{Z}$ and any $p\in V$ there is an open simply-connected set $W$ such that $V\z\supset W\ni p$.

For instance, Euclidean space is reasonable;
it follows since any open ball in a Euclidean space is convex; in particular, contractible and therefore simply-connected.

Since any simply-connected set is path-connected, any reasonable space is locally path-connected and locally connected;
see \ref{sec:Locally path-connected spaces} and \ref{sec:Locally connected spaces}.
In particular, by \ref{obs:open-connected-component} we have that every connected component of a reasonable space is clopen.


\begin{thm}[!]{Exercise}\label{ex:reasonable}
Let $w\:\c{Y}\to \c{Z}$ be a covering.
Show the following:
\begin{subthm}{}
If $\c{Z}$ is reasonable then so is $\c{Y}$, and the restriction of $w$ to any connected component of $\c{Y}$ is a covering.
\end{subthm}

\begin{subthm}{}
If $w$ is surjective and $\c{Y}$ is reasonable, then so is $\c{Z}$.
\end{subthm}

\end{thm}

In this chapter we will sketch a proof of the following theorem.

\vskip-8mm
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}
& (\c{Y},y_0)\arrow[d, "w"]\\
(\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_1) \arrow[r, "u"]\arrow[ur, "v"] & (\c{Z},z_0)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{wrapfigure}

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:unicover}
Any reasonable connected space $\c{Z}$ admits a covering $u\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\z\to \c{Z}$ with a simply-connected total space $\tilde{\c{Z}}$.

Moreover such a covering has the following \index{universal property}\emph{universal property}:
given a connected pointed covering $w\:(\c{Y},y_0)\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ and any choice of point $\tilde z_1\in \tilde{\c{Z}}$ such that $u(\tilde z_1)\z=z_0$ there is a pointed covering $v\:(\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_1)\z\to (\c{Y},y_0)$ which makes the diagram commute.
\end{thm}

The first part of the theorem will be provided by \ref{thm:u-cover-exist}, and the second part will follow from \ref{ex:reasonable:v} and \ref{thm:lifting-criterion}; see \ref{sec:Lifts again}.

The theorem can be used together with \ref{thm:bijection-fiber} and \ref{ex:monodromy:isomorphism};
it is useful since most spaces that appear in applications are reasonable.

The covering $u$ as in the theorem is called \index{universal covering}\emph{universal}.
Note that the universal property can be applied to the covering $u\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\z\to \c{Z}$ with different choices of base points.
It defines a homeomorphism $h\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\selfup$ and all such homeomorphisms define a group action on $\tilde{\c{Z}}$, which meets the definition of a normal covering;
applying \ref{ex:monodromy:isomorphism}, we get the following.

\begin{thm}{Observation}
A universal covering of a connected reasonable space $\c{Z}$ is a normal $\pi_1\c{Z}$-covering.
\end{thm}

\begin{wrapfigure}[5]{r}{48 mm}
\vskip-6mm
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep={1em}, row sep={2em}]
(\c{X},x_0)\arrow[dr, "v"']\arrow[rr, "h", dashed]& &(\c{Y},y_0)\arrow[dl, "w"]\\
&(\c{Z},z_0)&
\end{tikzcd}
\end{wrapfigure}

Note that the covering $v$ in \ref{thm:unicover} is universal, and by the observation, it has to be normal.
This leads to the following classification of coverings up to equivalence.
Two pointed coverings $v\:(\c{X},x_0)\z\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ and $w\:(\c{Y},y_0)\z\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ are \index{equivalent coverings}\emph{equivalent} if there is a pointed homeomorphism $h\:(\c{X},x_0)\z\to(\c{Y},y_0)$ that makes the diagram commute.

\begin{thm}{Proposition}\label{prop:classification-coverings}
Let $\c{Z}$ be a connected reasonable space.
Then a connected pointed covering $v\:(\c{X},x_0)\z\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ is uniquely defined up to equivalence by the subgroup $H=\Im v_*<\pi_1(\c{Z},z_0)$.
\end{thm}

This classification follows from \ref{ex:induced-hom-covering:injective}, \ref{prop:monodromy-homo}, and \ref{thm:unicover}; we will not prove it formally, but it is a good exercise in concentration.

In the following two sections we show how the theorem might fail to be true for nonreasonable spaces.

Let us denote by $\c{H}$ the \index{Hawaiian earring}\emph{Hawaiian earring}; it is a topological space homeomorphic to the union of a nested sequence of circles in the plane shown in the picture;
the radii of circles converge to zero and the circles have one common point.


\begin{thm}{Claim}\label{clm:earring}
The Hawaiian earring $\c{H}$ is not reasonable;
it is path-connected, locally path-connected, but not locally simply-connected.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
It is straightforward to check path-connectedness and local path-connectedness of $\c{H}$.
Let us show that $\c{H}$ is not locally simply-connected.


Let $p$ be the common point of the circles in $\c{H}$.
An arbitrary neighborhood $V$ of $p$ contains a circle, say $S$.
Consider the map $r\:V\to S$ that is the identity on $S$ and maps $V\setminus S$ to $p$.
Note that $r$ is a retraction.

Recall that $S$ has a nontrivial fundamental group; see \ref{thm:pi1(S1)}.
By \ref{ex:retract*:0}, $V$ is not simply-connected.
Hence the statement follows.
\qeds

Construct a continuous bijection $\c{F}_\infty\to\c{H}$.
Show that $\c{F}_\infty\not\simeq \c{H}$.

\end{thm}

\section{Reasonable coverings}

From now on we restrict attention to reasonable spaces.
The following claim is one of their key features.
Note that it does not hold for the Polish circle, so the claim does not hold without assuming reasonability.
(This time local path-connectedness will suffice.)

\begin{thm}{Claim}\label{clm:sc-base}
Let $w\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\z\to \c{Z}$ be a covering map.
Assume $\c{Z}$ is a reasonable simply-connected space.
Then $w$ is trivial.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
By \ref{ex:reasonable}, $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is reasonable, so each connected component of $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is clopen, and
it is sufficient to show that the restriction of $w$ to any connected component of $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is a homeomorphism.

In other words, we need to show that $w$ is a homeomorphism, assuming that $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is connected.

By \ref{thm:covering-open}, $w$ is open.
By \ref{ex:covering-clopen}, $w(\tilde{\c{Z}})$ is a clopen subset of $\c{Z}$.
Since $\c{Z}$ is simply-connected, $w$ is surjective.

It remains to show that $w$ is injective;
indeed, an open bijective continuous map must be a homeomorphism.

Choose two points $\tilde z_0,\tilde z_1\in\tilde{\c{Z}}$ that map to a single point, say $z_0\z=w(\tilde z_0)=w(\tilde z_1)$.
Since $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is path-connected, we can choose a path $\tilde a$ from $\tilde z_0$ to $\tilde z_1$.
Note that $a=w\circ \tilde a$ is a loop in $\c{Z}$.
Since $\c{Z}$ is simply-connected, $a\sim e_{z_0}$.
Now, \ref{lem:path-lifting} implies that $\tilde z_0=\tilde a(0)=\tilde a(1)=\tilde z_1$, which proves that $w$ is injective.
\qeds

Note that the statement at the end of this proof also follows from \ref{ex:induced-hom-covering:lift}.

\section{Lifts again}\label{sec:Lifts again}

The following theorem and \ref{ex:reasonable:v} provide the second part of \ref{thm:unicover}; that is, a covering of a connected reasonable space with a simply-connected total space must meet the universal property.

{

\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{36 mm}
\vskip-0mm
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}
& (\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_0)\arrow[d, "w"]\\
{(\c{Y},y_0)} \arrow[r, "f"']\arrow[ur, "g", dashed] & (\c{Z},z_0)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{wrapfigure}

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:lifting-criterion}
Let $w\:(\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_0)\z\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ be a pointed covering, and let $f\:(\c{Y},y_0)\z\to (\c{Z},z_0)$ be a continuous pointed map.
Assume $\c{Y}$ is reasonable and simply-connected.
Then there exists a unique pointed lift $g\:(\c{Y},y_0)\z\to (\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_0)$.
\end{thm}

}


Before reading the proof, refresh the definition of fibered product from \ref{sec:Fibered product}.

{

\begin{wrapfigure}[5]{r}{36 mm}
\vskip-6mm
\centering
\begin{tikzcd}
(\tilde{\c{Y}},\tilde y_0)\arrow[d, "\tilde w"'] \arrow[r, "\tilde f"] & (\tilde{\c{Z}},\tilde z_0) \arrow[d, "w"]\\
(\c{Y},y_0) \arrow[r, "f"'] \arrow[u, bend right=50, "s"', dotted]
 & (\c{Z},z_0)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{wrapfigure}

\parit{Proof.} The uniqueness follows from \ref{ex:unique-lift}.


Consider the fibered product of $f$ and $w$, described by the diagram.
By \ref{ex:pull-back}, $\tilde w\:\tilde{\c{Y}}\z\to \c{Y}$ is a covering.
Since $\c{Y}$ is reasonable and simply-connected, by \ref{clm:sc-base}, $\tilde w$ is trivial.
In particular, there is a continuous pointed map $s\:(\c{Y},y_0)\z\to (\tilde{\c{Y}},\tilde y_0)$ such that $\tilde w\circ s=\id_{\c{Y}}$ (the map $s$ is called a pointed \index{section}\emph{section} of $\tilde w$).

}

Note that the diagram commutes except $s\circ \tilde w\ne \id_{\tilde{\c{Y}}}$.
It remains to observe that $g=\tilde f\circ s$ is the required lift of $f$.
\qeds

\section{Existence}

\begin{thm}{Theorem}\label{thm:u-cover-exist}
Any connected reasonable space $\c{Z}$ admits a covering $u\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\to\c{Z}$ with simply-connected total space $\tilde{\c{Z}}$.
\end{thm}

\parit{Sketch of proof.}
We will construct a \textit{set} $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ with a map $u\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\to\c{Z}$, and then equip $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ with a topology and check that $u$ meets the conditions in the theorem.

\parit{The set and the map.}
Choose a point $z_0\in \c{Z}$.
Denote by $\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$ the set of all paths that start at $z_0$;
that is, a path $a\:[0,1]\to \c{Z}$ belongs to $\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$ if $a(0)=z_0$.

Let $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ be the set of all homotopy classes in $\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$; that is,
\[\tilde{\c{Z}}=\Path(\c{Z},z_0)/\sim,\]
where $\sim$ stands for the equivalence relation defined by the homotopy of paths;
see \ref{sec:Homotopy of paths}.
Recall that if $a_0\sim a_1$ for $a_0, a_1\z\in \Path(\c{Z},z_0)$, then
$a_0(1)=a_1(1)$.
Therefore, $u\:[a]\mapsto a(1)$ is a well-defined map $u\:\tilde{\c{Z}}\to\c{Z}$.


\parit{The topology.}
So far $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is just a set; this step equips it with a topology.

Let $V\subset \c{Z}$ be an open simply-connected set.
Choose a path $a\z\in\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$ that ends in $V$.
Consider the set $\tilde V_{[a]}\subset \tilde{\c{Z}}$ defined by
\[\tilde V_{[a]}=\set{[a*b]\in\tilde{\c{Z}}}{b\ \text{is a path in}\ V\ \text{that starts at}\ a(1)}.\]
Note that $\tilde V_{[a]}$ depends only on the homotopy class of $a$.

The required topology on $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ can be described by the following property:
for any open simply-connected set $V\subset \c{Z}$, and any path $a$ from $z_0$ to a point in $V$,
the set $\tilde V_{[a]}$ is open and $u$ defines a homeomorphism $\tilde V_{[a]}\to V$.%
\footnote{In this case, the sets $\tilde V_{[a]}$ for every open simply-connected set $V\subset \c{Z}$ and every path $a\in\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$ that ends in $V$ form a base of the topology on $\tilde{\c{Z}}$.}
Checking that this property uniquely defines a topology is generously left to the reader.

If $a$ and $b$ are two paths from $z_0$ to a point in $V$, then
\[\tilde V_{[a]}\cap\tilde V_{[b]}\ne\emptyset
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\tilde V_{[a]}=\tilde V_{[b]};\]
again, the proof is left to the reader.
In particular, $V$ is evenly covered by $u$ and the sets $\tilde V_{[a]}$ are the slices over $V$.
Since $\c{Z}$ is reasonable, it admits an open cover by simply-connected sets, and this implies that $u$ is a covering.

Let $\tilde z_0=[e_{z_0}]$; that is, the point $\tilde z_0\in \tilde{\c{Z}}$ corresponds to the constant path $e_{z_0}$ in $\c{Z}$.
Note that $u(\tilde z_0)=e_{z_0}(1)=z_0$.

Given a path $a$ and $s\in [0,1]$, let $a_s\:[0,1]\to \c{Z}$ be the path defined by $a_s(t)=a(s\cdot t)$;
note that $a_s$ runs from $a(0)$ to $a(s)$.%
\footnote{Note that $a_s$ is not a homotopy of paths, but it is a free homotopy; see \ref{sec:Homotopy of paths}.}

Choose $\tilde z=[a]\in \tilde{\c{Z}}$; here $a\in\Path(\c{Z},z_0)$.
By \ref{lem:path-lifting}, there is a unique lift $\tilde a$ of $a$ such that $\tilde a(0)=\tilde z_0$.
Observe that $\tilde a(s)\z= [a_s]$ for any $s\in[0,1]$.
In particular, $\tilde a$ is a path from $\tilde z_0$ to $\tilde z$;
since $\tilde z$ is arbitrary, we get that $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is path-connected.

Choose a loop $\tilde a\:[0,1]\to \tilde{\c{Z}}$ based at $\tilde z_0$.
Let $a=u\circ \tilde a$; it is a loop based at $z_0$.
From above, we have $\tilde a(s)= [a_s]$.
Note that $\tilde a$ is a loop, $a=a_1$ and $e_{z_0}=a_0$; therefore,
\[[a]=\tilde a(1)=\tilde a(0)=[e_{z_0}].\]
In other words, $a\sim e_{z_0}$.
By \ref{lem:path-lifting}, the corresponding homotopy can be lifted to $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ and it contracts $\tilde a$.
That is, any loop in $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is contractible; by \ref{prop:sc=1hclass}, $\tilde{\c{Z}}$ is simply-connected.
\qeds

Recall that topological groups were defined in \ref{ex:group}.

\begin{thm}{Exercise}\label{ex:group-unicover}
Let $u\:\tilde{\c{G}}\to \c{G}$ be the universal covering of a reasonable topological group $\c{G}$.
Show that $\tilde{\c{G}}$ has the structure of a topological group such that $u$ becomes a homomorphism of groups; that is, $u(\tilde g\cdot \tilde h)=u(\tilde g)\cdot u(\tilde h)$ for any $\tilde g$ and $\tilde h$ in $\tilde{\c{G}}$.
\end{thm}







\section{An application}

A \index{graph}\emph{graph} is a topological space built from a discrete set of points called vertices and copies of the interval $[0,1]$ called edges, where each edge is attached by identifying its two endpoints with vertices.

Groups that are isomorphic to the fundamental groups of graphs are called \index{free group}\emph{free}.

Free groups are important in group theory.
They are usually defined in purely algebraic terms.
The following statement, if proved in a purely algebraic way, is not trivial, but as we are about to see, a topological proof takes a few lines.
In a perfect world, I should present both definitions and prove their equivalence, but I will skip this part --- please forgive this shortcut.


\begin{thm}{Theorem}
Any subgroup of a free group is free.
\end{thm}

\parit{Proof.}
Choose a free group $F=\pi_1(\Gamma,v_0)$, where $\Gamma$ is a graph and $v_0$ is one of its vertices.
Note that any graph is a reasonable space.
By \ref{prop:classification-coverings}, for any subgroup $H<F$ there is a pointed covering $w\:(\tilde\Gamma,\tilde v_0)\z\to (\Gamma,v_0)$ such that $\Im w_*=H$.
Recall that $w_*$ is a monomorphism; thus $\pi_1(\tilde \Gamma,\tilde v_0)$ is isomorphic to $H$.

\vskip-0mm
\centering
\includegraphics{mppics/pic-1931}
\end{figure}

Observe that the total space of a covering of a graph is a graph.
Hence the statement follows.
\qeds

This statement gives some taste of possible applications of coverings in group theory.
For more on the subject, read \cite{hatcher-2002} and check the references therein.
